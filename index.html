🌍 What Is Carbon Emission, and Why Keep It Low?

Carbon emission (carbon footprint) is the total amount of greenhouse gases — mainly CO₂ — released into the atmosphere because of an activity, product, or service. Almost everything we spend money on (travel, food, electricity, shopping) causes some emissions during its production, transport, or use.

How this app calculates it:

Carbon (kg CO₂e) = Amount Spent (₹) × Carbon Factor of that Category

Every category has a carbon factor — an estimate of how many kg of CO₂e are emitted per ₹1 spent in it (e.g. transport & travel tend to be carbon-heavy, while bills or entertainment are usually lower). You can view and set this factor for any category you create yourself on the "Add Income / Expense" tab.

Why it matters to keep it minimum:

  • Lower emissions mean a smaller personal contribution to climate change and global warming.
  • Cleaner air and better health outcomes, especially in crowded cities.
  • Low-carbon habits (walking/cycling, home-cooked meals, mindful electricity use) are usually cheaper too — so it helps your savings goal at the same time.
  • Small, consistent reductions add up to a large impact over a month and a year.

Why food isn't just "one number": different foods have very different footprints for the same money spent — mainly because of how they're farmed (methane from cattle/rice paddies, feed & land use, refrigeration & transport). That's why "Add a Transaction" now lets you pick what kind of food it was for a more accurate estimate. Rough real-world figures (kg CO₂e per kg of food, from published lifecycle studies):

🥬 Fruits & vegetables~0.4 kg
🌾 Grains, rice & bread~1.5–2.7 kg
🥛 Milk & dairy~3 kg
🥚 Eggs & chicken~4.5–6 kg
🐟 Fish & seafood~5–6 kg
🥩 Red meat (mutton/beef)~25–27 kg

These are per kilogram of food, for illustration — the app's own factors (per ₹1 spent) preserve this same ranking so your Food category reflects it.
